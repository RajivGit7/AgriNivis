Method

How the score is built.

Each crop is compared with your field on eight weighted criteria. The weights favour the factors that most often decide whether a crop can be grown at all: season, soil and water.

What data goes into the recommendation?
Soil type, pH, soil moisture, irrigation availability, previous crop, fertilizer practice and season come from your inputs. Temperature, humidity and 7-day rainfall come from the free Open-Meteo forecast API when you provide a location. The crop suitability ranges and rotation relationships are stored in a reviewed JavaScript knowledge base, so the scoring runs entirely in your browser.
Why a rule-based engine instead of a trained model?
Public crop-recommendation datasets usually contain only N, P, K, temperature, humidity, pH and rainfall. They do not include previous crop, irrigation reliability or local soil type, which farmers can actually report. A transparent rule-based engine can use all of those inputs, explain every point in the score, and be audited by an agronomist. The disease classifier is the trained ML component of AgriNivis; the recommender is a complementary decision-support layer.
What are the main limitations?
The crop ranges are broad and intended for screening. The engine does not know your soil test values, variety, seed cost, labour availability or market price. Weather is a short forecast, not a seasonal climate prediction. Always validate the shortlist against local package of practices, and treat the output as one input into your decision.
